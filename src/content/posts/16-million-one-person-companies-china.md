**16 MILLION ONE-PERSON COMPANY IN CHINA 🇨🇳**

I recently exchanged notes with Stephanie Lin on one-person companies, my latest The Future Is Solo (TFIS) thesis, and the roadmap towards 2030.

I told her that the most interesting solo-builders I'd kept bumping into are: restarting their life at 40.

… "Over 40. Non-technical. First time builders."

That's exactly the audience I'm building TFIS for.

My bet: some of the most overlooked future founders are people with decades of experience—and no idea how to write code.

They know which problems keep coming back. Which workarounds customers tolerate. Which things people will actually pay to fix.

What's missing isn't necessarily an idea.

It's a way to turn that knowledge into a business without assembling an entire team first.

What matters to me:

A one-person company isn't one person doing ten people's jobs. It's one person designing a system so they don't have to.

I call that person a Solo Systems Architect (SSA): someone who combines their judgment, AI and repeatable workflows to deliver something customers value.

But buying AI subscriptions doesn't make you one.

You still need to understand the customer. Make the economics work. Check the outputs. Own the consequences.

That's why my work includes a maturity framework for solo builders, alongside a fractional Chief AI Officer model: part-time leadership to help founders build and govern their AI systems, then run them independently.

Not permanent dependence on an expert. Capability that stays with the founder.

Solo doesn't mean isolated. Collaboration still matters.

And experience alone isn't enough. You still have to learn, test and sell.

But being non-technical shouldn't automatically disqualify you from building.

Your next company might begin with a problem you've understood for 20 years. Not a technology you discovered last week.

What problem from your working life would you finally build a business to solve?
